## More than a tower with an antenna

A repeater is a radio receiver and transmitter placed up high — on a
tower, a mountain, or a tall building — that listens on one frequency
and instantly retransmits what it hears on another.

Because it's up high with a good antenna and more power than a typical
handheld radio, a repeater can cover an entire city or county. Two
people with small handheld radios who couldn't reach each other directly
can both reach the repeater — and suddenly they're talking.

## How it works (the short version)

1. You set your radio to the repeater's frequency pair.
2. When you press the transmit button, your radio automatically shifts
   to the repeater's *input* frequency.
3. The repeater hears you and rebroadcasts your voice on its *output*
   frequency — which everyone in range can hear.
4. When you release the button, you hear the repeater's output again,
   ready for the next person.

All of this happens in real time, with no delay you'd notice.

## Why repeaters matter

- **Range:** A 5-watt handheld might reach a few miles on its own. Through
  a repeater, it can cover 30 miles or more.
- **Accessibility:** You don't need a big antenna or expensive equipment
  to get on the air locally.
- **Community:** Most repeaters host regular *nets* — scheduled on-air
  gatherings where people check in, share news, and practice operating.
- **Emergencies:** When cell towers are down, a repeater with backup
  power can keep a community connected.

## KARC's repeaters

The Kingsport Amateur Radio Club operates repeaters on Bays Mountain.
They're open to all licensed amateurs.

Want to listen? You can with a inexpensive scanner or an SDR receiver —
no license needed to listen. To talk, you'll need an amateur radio
license.
