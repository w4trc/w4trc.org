## A license opens the door

Once you have your amateur radio license and callsign, a whole range of
activities opens up. Here's a sampling — and you don't have to do all of
them. Most hams find a few they love and dive deep.

## Talk locally

- **Repeaters:** The easiest way to get on the air. Use a handheld radio
  to talk across your city or county through a mountaintop repeater.
- **Simplex:** Direct radio-to-radio contact — no repeater needed. Good
  for events, car-to-car, or just keeping it simple.
- **Nets:** Scheduled on-air gatherings. Check in, say hello, practice
  procedures.

## Reach around the world

- **HF / DXing:** The high-frequency bands can bounce signals off the
  ionosphere and reach thousands of miles. Collecting contacts from
  distant countries — called *DXing* — is a lifelong pursuit for many.
- **Contesting:** Timed competitions to make as many contacts as possible.
  Fast-paced, social, and a great way to improve your operating skills.

## Go digital

- **Text and email over radio** using modes like FT8, JS8Call, and
  Winlink — no internet connection needed.
- **APRS:** Automatic position reporting. Your radio sends your GPS
  location, and you can see other stations on a map in real time.
- **Digital voice:** DMR, D-STAR, and System Fusion connect handheld
  radios to worldwide talk groups through the internet.

## Head outdoors

- **Parks on the Air (POTA) / Summits on the Air (SOTA):** Activate a
  park or summit by setting up a portable station and making contacts.
  Or hunt activators from home.
- **Field Day:** The biggest event of the year — clubs set up temporary
  stations outdoors and operate for 24 hours. Part contest, part
  emergency drill, part cookout.
- **Fox hunting:** Hide a transmitter and use directional antennas to
  find it. Part radio, part orienteering.

## Build and experiment

- **Antenna building:** Design and construct your own antennas — one of
  the most practical and rewarding parts of the hobby.
- **Kit building:** Assemble radios, tuners, and accessories from kits.
- **SDR:** Software-defined radio lets you see and decode radio signals
  on a computer screen.
- **Satellites:** Yes, you can talk through amateur radio satellites —
  even the International Space Station — with portable equipment.

## Serve your community

- **Public service:** Provide communication for races, parades, and
  community events.
- **Emergency communications:** ARES, RACES, and SKYWARN volunteers
  relay critical information during severe weather and disasters.
- **License classes and mentoring:** Help the next group of hams get
  licensed and on the air.

## Where to start

Most people start with a handheld radio and a local repeater — it's
affordable, immediate, and connects you to the club. From there, follow
what interests you.
