Sometime in November, I received the text "The repeaters are down". I got to a radio, keyed them up, and sure enough nothing worked right. I let our repeater team know and we began coordinating a trip up Bays Mountain to investigate.

We require a team of 2 people anytime we go up the mountain for safety reasons, especially when dealing with power. So a few days later when we got a team together, we made the trip up the mountain and found some weird power issues.

When we got there, we noticed the main 2-meter repeater would repeatedly reboot and not work properly. Even weirder we noticed that when turning the repeaters off - the lights would flicker. We then knew it was a problem with a neutral wire somewhere. We took breaker panels apart and found some old wiring in a disconnect outside of the shack. This had to be our culprit. 

** photo of wiring

In an abundance of caution, we shut everything down and disconnected it from the power. At that point, the repeaters were offline and did not work. You may be asking "I thought we have solar backups". That is the question we asked to. I know we have had solar backups because we swapped the solar controller and battery not long ago, but that system was not working.

Through some more investigation, we discovered the cause of this. An exposed connection that had corroded and made the solar panels not charge the system. 

Now we knew everything that was going on so we had to make a plan of fixing it. We got off the mountain and had a meeting with a few people and made plans of what equipment needed to be replaced.

We had to wait a few weeks for weather, scheduling of our team, and scheduling of AEP to come disconnect the power for us to work safely. Once all of this was done and the parts were purchased we had everything we needed.

We made another trip up the mountain, this time to replace some wiring. We had one team working on replacing the main disconnect and housing while another team worked on the solar backup system.

The AC wiring was in bad shape from the transformer to the shack so we replaced it all. New wires, new conduit, new breaker, new meter base, and new outlets outside.

The solar panel wiring was replaced from the solar panels on the roof to the solar charge controller. We also replaced the single battery which had died with 2 brand new ones. 

Due to AEP scheduling, we had to run off of battery power for a week, but this was a good test of our backup system and it performed perfectly. We never noticed a single drop. The new batteries should hold the repeater online for days without sunshine.

Once AEP came and reconencted our service, we were able to turn everything back online and thankfully it all worked.

Now the repeaters are back online for full time use.
