On October 8, 2026, Larry Dale, KD4FTN, shared photos and stories from a family trip to Connecticut — including a four-hour visit to W1AW, the ARRL's headquarters station, which he calls "the holy grail of ham radio."

## Two buildings, one legendary station

W1AW is split between two buildings: the original building and a newer, modern one. Visitors start in the new building, which houses the lobby, a store, a museum area, and the ARRL lab. The studios where visitors can get on the air are in the old building across the street.

Larry was struck by the antenna farm. Everything looked professionally planned and installed, right down to the grounding, and he noted that nothing about it looked like it came out of somebody's garage.

## The museum

The museum area is full of amateur radio history, including:

- Vintage Collins gear, the same equipment Larry remembers from a MARS station during his military service
- Old shortwave receivers like the one he listened to as a kid, which is how he got into ham radio
- A spark-gap transmitter associated with Hiram Percy Maxim, co-founder of the ARRL. It has been restored to the point that it still transmits.
- Display cases of HF and VHF ham kits

Larry also remarked on how spotless the building is: every piece of equipment in its place, and not a speck of dust anywhere.

## The lab

The ARRL lab is where the equipment reviews in QST come from. Larry noted that, for some of what they measure, the lab has had to invent its own test equipment because nothing on the market does the job. He credited donations of money and equipment from companies like DX Engineering for helping ARRL keep the best gear on hand.

## Operating from W1AW

Larry spent four hours at the facility, which the staff told him was longer than any visitor had stayed. Everyone who works there is a ham, and once the staff warmed up to the group they showed them things most visitors never see.

The old building has several operating studios, each set up with two complete stations. Visiting hams can operate for about an hour, and everything goes out under the W1AW callsign. Larry made one contact on 20 meters, with Bob Warden, and played a recording of it for the club. Operating took a little getting used to, since the stations use a foot pedal to transmit.

## Also on the trip

Larry rounded out the presentation with a few stops along the way:

- **The Pez factory**, with a wall of collectible dispensers and a highly automated production line
- **The USS Nautilus**, the first nuclear-powered submarine, commissioned in 1954 and in service until 1980. It was also the first submarine to travel under the North Pole.
- **The Mystic River bascule bridge** in Mystic, Connecticut, built in 1922 and still opening for boat traffic

He also encouraged members to visit the radio museum in Asheville if they get the chance, noting it has added rooms since the club last visited.
